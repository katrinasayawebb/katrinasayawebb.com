## The why

*What problem was worth solving?*

Estimating incremental campaign spend meant manually filtering line-item budgets, calculating budget-increase scenarios, and consolidating the results in Excel.

The goal was to automate that repetitive analysis while keeping Excel as the deliverable, since that's what business stakeholders actually open.

**Success:** consistent line-item and campaign-level budget scenarios, with minimal manual intervention.

## The work

*What did I build, and how?*

A reusable Python workflow, built with pandas and openpyxl, that turns a platform budget export into incremental spend scenarios. The script:

- Imports monthly flight budgets from Excel.
- Identifies each line item's channel from its naming convention (`Disp`, `Vid`, `CTV`).
- Filters for Display and online video budgets in November and December.
- Accounts for a platform-specific date discrepancy affecting November budgets.
- Calculates a conservative (+25%) and an aggressive (+50%) budget scenario.
- Aggregates the results by month and channel.
- Exports a workbook with a campaign summary and line-item detail.

**Technical decisions:** a dictionary mapping for channel classification, pandas for filtering and aggregation, and openpyxl for Excel formatting.

## The win

*What worked, and how do I know?*

A repeatable workflow that converts a platform export into a structured incremental budget analysis. It produces:

- A campaign-level summary by month and channel.
- A line-item budget breakout.
- Conservative and aggressive incremental spend scenarios.
- A script that needs only a campaign ID and an input file to run again.

## The lesson

*What did I learn?*

**Technical:** pandas DataFrame operations, dictionary mapping, date transformations, `groupby()` aggregation, and Excel automation.

**Debugging:** how to diagnose `KeyError` exceptions, normalize inconsistent Excel headers, and keep source data separate from reporting transformations.

**Tradeoff:** a budget-increase calculator is not the same as a validated model of incremental spend capacity. This workflow models hypothetical increases; it doesn't establish whether the additional inventory can actually be bought efficiently.

**Next iteration:** bring in historical delivery, bid rates, win rates, and auction availability, moving from budget scenarios to evidence-based incremental spend recommendations.
